{\small\tightdisplay
\topic{Pick's theorem}
To count the lattice points inside a polygon whose vertices are lattice points:
\[ A = I + \frac{B}{2} - 1, \]
\[ B = \sum_{\text{edges}} \gcd(|\Delta x|, |\Delta y|). \]
$A$ is the area and $I$ the number of lattice points strictly inside. $B$ is the number on the border.
Example: the triangle $(0, 0), (4, 0), (0, 4)$ has $A = 8$, $B = 12$, $I = 3$.
\topic{Triangle from its sides}
To work with side lengths $a, b, c$ instead of coordinates, let $p = (a + b + c) / 2$:
\[ A = \sqrt{p(p - a)(p - b)(p - c)}, \]
\[ R = \frac{abc}{4A}, \]
\[ r = \frac{A}{p}, \]
\[ m_a = \tfrac{1}{2} \sqrt{2b^2 + 2c^2 - a^2}, \]
\[ l_a = \sqrt{bc \left(1 - \left(\frac{a}{b + c}\right)^2\right)}. \]
$A$ is the area. $R$ and $r$ are the radii of the circumscribed and the inscribed circle. $m_a$ and $l_a$ are the median and the angle bisector to side $a$.
}
\cpp[4]{Convex hull}{convexhull.hpp}[
	\fld{Use} \texttt{auto h = convexHull(pts); auto [a, b] = hullDiameter(h);}
	\fld{Result} \texttt{h} has at most 2 points when the input points are collinear.
	\fld{Needs} a non-empty \texttt{h} from \texttt{convexHull} for \texttt{hullDiameter} and \texttt{inHull}.
	\fld{hullDiameter} returns points, not indices; map them back for indices.
	\fld{inHull} With \texttt{strict = false} the border is inside; a hull of 1 or 2 points is then a point or a segment.
]\label{convexhull}
